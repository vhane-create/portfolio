# October accounting close memorandum

**To:** Management, Atlas Desk Products LLC (fictional)  
**Prepared for:** Vannetta Haney’s accounting portfolio  
**Period:** October 1–31, 2026  
**Basis:** USD; accrual accounting; perpetual inventory; FIFO. All data and evidence identifiers are simulated.

## Conclusion

After recording all supported adjustments, October revenue is **$47,000**, net income is **$14,790**, and ending cash is **$44,250**. Total assets are **$141,990** and equal liabilities plus equity. Operating cash inflow is **$1,250**. Profit is positive, but gross accounts receivable increased by $20,000 to $32,000; collection activity deserves attention before committing more cash.

## Entries and judgment

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit | Baseline amount | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| A01 | Insurance expense | Prepaid insurance | $500 | One month of a $6,000 annual policy has expired. |
| A02 | Depreciation expense | Accumulated depreciation | $800 | Existing office equipment costs $48,000 and uses 60-month straight-line depreciation with no residual value. The $6,000 addition enters service November 1. |
| A03 | Office salaries | Wages payable | $1,200 | October labor is incurred but unpaid. |
| A04 | Bad debt expense | Allowance for doubtful accounts | $960 | Illustrative aging-based estimate of 3% of $32,000 gross closing receivables. No opening allowance or write-offs. |
| A05 | Deferred service revenue | Service revenue | $1,000 | This portion of the $4,000 customer deposit has been earned. The remaining $3,000 stays a liability. |
| A06 | Cost of goods sold | Manufacturing overhead control | $500 | Actual overhead of $5,500 exceeds applied overhead of $5,000. The variance is assumed immaterial and expensed. A material variance would require a separate allocation assessment. |
| A07 | Bank fees | Cash | $100 | Fee appears on the bank statement but was not in the ledger. |
| A08 | Cash | Interest income | $50 | Deposit interest appears on the bank statement but was not in the ledger. |

Omitting depreciation still leaves a balanced trial balance and balance sheet; it overstates profit and net assets by $800. A close-completeness review must therefore accompany arithmetic tie-outs.

## Inventory and margin

Opening finished goods contain 100 units at $80 each. October production adds 200 units: direct materials $9,000, direct labor $6,000 and applied overhead $5,000. Production cost is $20,000, or $100 per unit. At 230 units sold, FIFO cost is $8,000 from the opening layer plus $13,000 from current production. Ending finished goods are 70 units at $100, totaling $7,000.

After the $500 overhead adjustment, product cost of goods sold is $21,500 and product gross profit is $24,500 on $46,000 sales: **53.26% product gross margin**. Service revenue is reported separately and is excluded from this product-margin measure. The service work has no separately modeled incremental direct cost.

## Cash reconciliation

The bank statement ends at $46,150. Add $3,000 deposits in transit and deduct $4,900 outstanding checks to obtain $44,250 adjusted bank cash. The unadjusted book cash of $44,300 falls by the $100 fee and rises by $50 interest, arriving at the same $44,250. Deposits in transit and outstanding checks are already recorded in the books; they do not receive duplicate journal entries.

The indirect cash-flow statement starts with $14,790 net income, adds $800 depreciation and $960 allowance expense, and incorporates operating working-capital changes. It reconciles to $1,250 direct operating cash flow. Equipment purchases use $6,000 and principal repayment uses $1,000. Opening cash $50,000 + $1,250 − $6,000 − $1,000 = ending cash $44,250. Interest paid and received are classified as operating in this case.

## Management recommendation

1. Prioritize collections on the $32,000 gross receivables balance and validate the allowance with a documented aging review.
2. Investigate the $500 overhead variance and review the normal-capacity allocation before reusing the $100 production unit cost for pricing.
3. Retain the adjustment evidence and bank reconciliation with the close record. A zero accounting-equation difference alone does not establish a complete or correct close.
