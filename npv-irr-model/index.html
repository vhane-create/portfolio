03 / Model design

Return is only as useful
as its assumptions.

Designed to demonstrate financial model construction, review and capital allocation reasoning.

01

Build incremental cash flow

Revenue less cost of sales and operating cost gives EBITDA. Deduct straight-line depreciation for EBIT, calculate cash taxes, then add depreciation back and deduct maintenance capex and incremental working capital.

02

Get the timing right

Year 0 is immediate. Years 1–5 occur at year-end. Excel uses NPV(rate, Year 1:Year 5) + Year 0. IRR includes the complete Year 0–Year 5 cash flow series.

03

Test value and resilience

Base, Downside and Upside cases change business drivers. Excel includes a native two-input What-If Data Table linking revenue multiplier and discount rate to the same cash flow model.

04

Explain the limits

Five-year, finite-life project. No perpetuity or financing. Initial capex depreciates to zero. Maintenance is cash-only for this simplified model. Tax losses receive no current benefit, and tax-loss carryforwards are excluded.

What a hiring manager can review

  • Editable assumptions and formula-driven Excel calculations.
  • Working capital funding, annual changes and final recovery.
  • After-tax disposal proceeds and the difference between earnings and cash.
  • NPV sensitivity, year-end payback and IRR limitations.
  • Documented methods, independent calculation checks and runnable source tests.

NPV measures modeled dollar value at the chosen hurdle. IRR is shown only for an initial outflow followed by nonnegative inflows. Unconventional cash flows can have multiple or no IRRs. Payback ignores discounting and is supplementary.
